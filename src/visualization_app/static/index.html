1. The Hyperspectral Advantage
Unlike regular 3-channel RGB cameras, hyperspectral imaging captures hundreds of contiguous optical wavelengths (400–2500 nm). Every pixel contains a continuous spectral fingerprint that distinguishes delicate crop varieties, vegetation health, and soil minerals.
⏳
2. The Annotation Bottleneck
Manually labeling satellite imagery requires expensive ground-truth surveys and remote-sensing experts. Traditional random sampling wastes costly human effort by labeling redundant pixels deep inside uniform crop fields that the model already knows.
🎯
3. The Pure Margin Solution
Our system measures the probability gap between the model's top two class predictions: Δp = p(1) − p(2). When this gap is tiny, the model is deeply uncertain. Pure Margin queries only these critical boundary pixels, reducing annotation workload by 11.7% to 23.6%.

Evaluation of Candidate Active Learning Strategies

Transparent audit of what we considered, what experiments showed, and why Pure Margin was selected

Pure Margin Sampling
Primary Proposed
u(x) = 1 − (p(1) − p(2))
What It Does
Measures the gap between the two highest predicted class probabilities. When this gap is tiny, the sample sits directly on the decision boundary.
Why We Considered It
Boundary samples provide the maximum gradient signal to refine the classifier head without needing arbitrary threshold tuning.
Experimental Findings
Achieved highest final accuracy across all scenes (70.15% IP, 90.24% SA, 77.85% PU) and highest label savings (21.9%–22.4%).
✓ RETAINED: Selected as our primary active learning acquisition algorithm.
Passive Random Sampling
Standard Baseline
x ∼ Uniform(U)
What It Does
Uniformly samples candidate pixels without any model uncertainty or feature guidance.
Why We Considered It
Universal baseline across all machine learning literature to prove that active learning provides non-random value.
Experimental Findings
Suffers from redundancy, wasting labels on uniform parcel interiors. Requires 65–70 more labels to reach target accuracy.
✓ RETAINED AS BASELINE: Used to compute label savings and statistical significance.
Least Confidence
Discarded
u(x) = 1 − p(1)
What It Does
Queries samples where the top predicted probability is lowest, ignoring all remaining class probabilities.
Why We Considered It
Simplest classical uncertainty sampling metric in early literature.
Experimental Findings
Fails to distinguish true boundary ambiguity (e.g. 51% vs 49%) from multi-class noise (e.g. 51% vs 10% each across 5 classes).
✗ DISCARDED: Replaced by Pure Margin which directly evaluates the second-best competitor.
LC + Farthest Diversity
Discarded
S(x) = wU uLC(x) + wD dFD(x)
What It Does
Combines least confidence with Euclidean feature distance to force spatial-spectral spread.
Why We Considered It
Attempted to avoid spatial clustering by penalizing candidate samples close to existing labeled points.
Experimental Findings
Underperformed Pure Margin by −6.75% OA on Indian Pines. The diversity term repeatedly selected uninformative outlier noise at image edges.
✗ DISCARDED: Heuristic diversity weighting harmed classification accuracy.

Protocol A: Standard Random-Pixel Partition

Uniform random sampling across spatial coordinates. While standard across published active learning benchmarks, neighboring pixels often share spatial correlation.

Protocol B: Spatially Disjoint Block Partition

Enforces strict spatial block separation with a 7-pixel guard band (15-pixel receptive field margin), guaranteeing zero spatial autocorrelation leakage between training and testing sets.

Subsection B: Scientific Limitations & Forensic Failure Analyses

Transparent academic disclosure of assumptions, physical boundaries, and negative results

1. Cross-Sensor Missing SWIR Absorption Bands & Negative Transfer
Observation: Statistically significant negative transfer (ΔOA = −1.08% at N=150, p=0.0087) from AVIRIS (Salinas) to ROSIS (Pavia University).
Physical Cause: AVIRIS covers 400–2500 nm (VNIR-SWIR), whereas ROSIS is strictly confined to 430–860 nm (VNIR only). Pretraining on agricultural SWIR features (lignin, cellulose, water content) harms performance when transferred to urban VNIR data without SWIR.
Resolution: Transfer latent representations directly while actively querying target samples with Pure Margin.
2. Failure of Historical 1D Nearest-Neighbor Clamping
Observation: Historical scripts clamped missing ROSIS channels above 860 nm to constant boundary values.
Physical Cause: Clamping created a flat, artificial reflectance plateau across 160 missing SWIR channels. 3D-CNN kernels produced spurious activations, destroying spatial-spectral features.
Resolution: Replaced with native foundation embeddings per sensor cube.
3. Disjoint Class Ontologies & Zero-Shot Collapse
Observation: Direct zero-shot application collapses to <13% OA.
Physical Cause: Agricultural classes (Corn, Soybeans) and urban classes (Asphalt, Bitumen) share zero taxonomic overlap. Zero-shot transfer is mathematically undefined without target active learning.
Resolution: Re-initialize classifier head (256 → C_tgt) and query target domain actively.
4. Spatial Autocorrelation & Guard Band Requirements
Observation: Random-pixel partitions inflate accuracy by 5–10% compared to spatially disjoint blocks.
Physical Cause: Hyperspectral imagery exhibits high spatial autocorrelation; training pixels adjacent to test pixels leak contextual information through spatial convolution kernels.
Resolution: Evaluated both Protocol A and Protocol B with 7-pixel guard bands for rigorous academic reporting.
5. Airborne Benchmark vs. Operational Orbital Satellite Boundary
Observation: All evaluations were conducted on airborne sensor cubes (AVIRIS & ROSIS).
Physical Cause: Operational spaceborne hyperspectral satellites (e.g., EnMAP, PRISMA, PACE) encounter lower spatial resolution (30m GSD), atmospheric absorption, and cloud occlusion.
Resolution: Explicitly documented as a research boundary; operational orbital deployment requires future satellite validation.
