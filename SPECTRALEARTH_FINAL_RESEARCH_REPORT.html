Adaptive Label-Efficient Cross-Sensor Hyperspectral Image Classification Framework Using Frozen Spectral Foundation Models

Dr. Arun Anoop M, Harinand Sreeshan, Ashik Krishnan, Mrinal Ramachandran, Arjun Sasi
Department of Computer Science and Engineering, AJ Institute of Engineering and Technology, Karnataka, India
Contact: harinandsreeshan@gmail.com | dr.arunanoop@ajiet.edu.in
Executive Summary & Abstract
Hyperspectral image (HSI) classification is a cornerstone of quantitative remote sensing. However, supervised deep models and vision transformers demand extensive manual pixel annotations and require computationally expensive full fine-tuning. On limited labels, fine-tuning large backbones leads to catastrophic forgetting, severe overfitting, and prediction miscalibration.

This report presents a complete Adaptive Label-Efficient Cross-Sensor Hyperspectral Classification Framework that freezes a pretrained hyperspectral foundation model (SpectralEarth, 24.24M parameters) and adapts only a lightweight 2-layer MLP classifier (533K parameters, only 2.15% of system parameters). Pre-computed 2048-dimensional embeddings are cached in memory, accelerating per-round feature extraction from 7.733s to 0.0919s (an 84.13x speedup, and up to 2845x per patch) with zero numerical discrepancy.

An active query engine couples validation temperature scaling with candidate-filtered farthest diversity traversal, delivering up to 23.1% label savings (sparing 60 annotations to reach 60% OA on Indian Pines) and reducing Expected Calibration Error (ECE) by 43.8% to 50.3%. A 90-run ablation suite proves that blindly stacking regularizers causes statistically significant degradation (p = 0.0288 on Salinas), confirming the superiority of a clean, calibrated query design. For cross-sensor transfer, deterministic 1D spectral resampling enables AVIRIS-to-ROSIS adaptation, achieving 49.84%–54.12% zero-shot OA and reaching 61.80% OA within 190 target annotations.

The framework decouples representation learning, classification, logit calibration, and query acquisition into two distinct engines: Model A (Learning Engine) and Model B (Query Engine).

4. Controlled Ablation & Negative Component Stacking Proof

A multi-factor ablation study across 90 runs proves that blindly stacking all potential additions (Dropout 0.1 + Class Weights + Labeled-Pool Diversity) degrades performance:

Dataset Comparison Full Method OA (%) Baseline OA (%) Difference t-statistic p-value Empirical Verdict
Indian Pines FULL vs Baseline A 61.24 61.26 -0.03% -0.0180 0.9866 Not Significant
Salinas FULL vs Baseline A 71.77 73.76 -1.98% -3.3420 0.0288 Significant Degradation (p < 0.05)
Pavia Univ. FULL vs Random 57.17 65.79 -8.62% -7.9650 0.0013 Severe Degradation (p < 0.01)
Theoretical Explanation of Negative Stacking:
The frozen foundation backbone already produces well-regularized representations. Adding dropout causes underfitting on small training pools. Combining class-balanced loss with dropout creates conflicting optimization gradients: class weights heavily penalize majority errors while dropout randomly zeros active units, destabilizing retraining. Therefore, the complex stacked pipeline is rejected, and Calibrated LC + Farthest Diversity (`Dropout = 0.0`) is retained as the primary universal method.
